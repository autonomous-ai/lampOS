Get your lamp working with your computer

Your lamp can hand work to the AI helpers on your computer, such as Claude Code. You ask out loud. The lamp tells you when it is done. A program called Harness carries the work from the lamp to your computer. Setup takes about ten minutes.

What you can ask your lamp to do

Ask for a piece of work.
“Ask Claude Code to write a poem about the rain.” The lamp repeats the task, you say yes, and it tells you Claude Code has it.
Ask for something from your work computer.
“Ask Claude Code on my office computer to look at why the build is slow and tell me what it finds.”
Hear how it went, without asking.
When the helper finishes, the lamp tells you at a good moment. If it stops to ask you something, the lamp tells you that too.

Nothing starts without your spoken yes. The lamp only hands work to the helper you name, and only to computers TO CHECK: phones.

Three steps

1Install the Harness app on your computer

Picture plan: the Harness download page with the button circled, then the app's first screen. TO CHECK: Harness's install page

2Sign in to Harness

Picture plan: the sign-in screen, then the signed-in home screen. Hide names and emails. TO CHECK: the account

3Connect Harness to your lamp

In the Autonomous app, open the Harness card and start. The app shows an 8-digit code. In Harness, open Machines, then Approve a device, and type it. The app then asks “Sign in as” with your email: choose yes if it is yours.

Program your lamp from your computer

Light it, make it speak, move it, hear its own phrases, see that someone arrived, from a program on your computer. About ten minutes.

What you get

Pairing. The owner pairs a computer once in the Autonomous app (Add a developer computer) and chooses what it may do. It can be revoked at any time. The person always wins: a hand on the head, “stop”, the privacy switch or a conversation takes control back.
The SDK. Python and JavaScript, the same calls: events, state and actions, each needing a grant. Plus a fake lamp for trying with no lamp.

Run an example

# install (from a checkout of the repository)
python3 -m venv ~/lampos-venv && . ~/lampos-venv/bin/activate
pip install ./lampOS/sdk/python

# terminal 1: a fake lamp
LAMPOS_HOME=/tmp/lampos-try lamp fake

# terminal 2: examples/first_light.py
LAMPOS_HOME=/tmp/lampos-try python first_light.py
connected to lamp-fake with ['body', 'light', 'speak', 'look', 'hear', 'sound', 'skills', 'air', 'device', 'camera', 'microphone']
lamp state: {'lamp': {'talking': False, 'paused': False, 'private': False, 'asleep': False}}

Ran against the fake lamp, not a real one.

Your real lamp

lamp pair 12345678 --pin <the 64 characters the app shows>
lamp hello
python first_light.py

The SDK checks the lamp's certificate against the pin before it sends anything.
