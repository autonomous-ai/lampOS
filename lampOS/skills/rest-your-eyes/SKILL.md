# Rest your eyes

When you have been at your desk for half an hour, at any hour, the lamp gives a small curious tilt, as if something across
the room caught its eye, and its light softens a shade for the three
seconds of the tilt: a cue to look up from the screen and rest your eyes on
something far away. It says one short line suggesting it. Then the light
is as it was.

The lamp sees faces, not screens: "screen time" here is time sitting at
the desk.

What it says and when:

- It says one short, light line, worded by the voice the way a friend
  would: no lecture, nothing about health.
- Again each 45 minutes while you stay, with no daily most; never while
  you read or write head down (with the eyes' activity on), and never in
  the middle of a conversation.
- With the eyes' activity on, the sitting is yours: someone else sitting down in
  your chair begins it again, so it is never theirs at your 30 minutes.
- No shared limit with the other wellbeing nudges, but never within a
  minute of another one's line: it waits that minute, then speaks.
- With Quiet focus on the lamp, as you look up after a long focus, Quiet
  focus's word no longer comes in its place (the owner, 27 Nov: no shared
  budget): both may come.
- "Not today" or "stop the eye breaks" to the lamp (one function for the
  wellbeing nudges: the one that came last, or all of them) snoozes it until
  tomorrow morning or turns it off until you ask for it again.

It keeps nothing about you: only, in memory, when it last went; off is one
line with its name.

A core skill: it comes with the lamp, on, and can be turned off by voice
or in the app.

If asked about it, the lamp says something like: "Every so often, when
you've been at the screen a while, I soften my light a moment and suggest
you look up and rest your eyes. Tell me 'not today' and I'll leave it."
