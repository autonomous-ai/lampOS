# Light out when you leave

When nobody has been at the lamp for forty minutes (no face seen, no voice
heard, no touch), its light dims a little and holds there for five minutes:
a soft cue to anyone still there. Then it goes out slowly, over about a
minute, as something falling asleep would. Out is the faint ember the lamp
always keeps, never dark: it still shows the lamp can hear.

The moment someone sits down in front of it, speaks to it, touches it or
pets it, the light is as it was again: the colour and the level the person
chose, which the lamp keeps. While it is dim, a movement in view (a page
turned by someone reading with their head down) brings it back as well, so
it never puts out the light on a reader. After the light has gone out,
a movement in view brings it back to the dim, not the full light, and it
waits five minutes again before it goes out. After a few such movements it
stays dim until someone comes back or the night begins, and at night a
movement never brings the light back. It does the same if the lamp stops
looking (the privacy switch, a mute): it never puts the light out on someone
it cannot see.

If anyone asks it to keep the light on, it does, for everyone, until someone
says it may turn the light off again. It does this once each time the desk is left. It keeps still at night (23:00
to 06:00, the lamp's local time), when the night is the lamp's sleep; if
nobody has been there since the night, the light dims at 06:00. It never
puts the light out in the middle of a conversation: if the forty minutes come
while the lamp is speaking, it waits for the lamp to finish.

If asked about it: "When everyone's gone I dim my light, then let it go out,
slowly, and I put it back the moment you sit down or touch me. If you'd rather I kept it on, say so."
