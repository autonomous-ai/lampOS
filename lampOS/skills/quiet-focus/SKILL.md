# Quiet focus

When you have been working head down at your desk, reading or writing,
for about 45 minutes in one sitting, the lamp turns to you and gives you
one small, warm word: that it noticed you've been focused a while, and
it's nicely done. It asks nothing of you and suggests nothing to do. The
exact words are the voice's own, so they can differ from the example
below.

What it says and when:

- After 45 minutes head down, as the eyes see it (the eyes' activity, on in
  the product). A glance up shorter than two minutes doesn't end the
  focus; a longer one does, and the 45 minutes begin again.
- Once a sitting at most, and twice a day, from 09:00 to 20:00: never in
  the evening or at night.
- Only when nobody else is in view: someone out of the camera's sight
  isn't seen, so it can still speak then. If someone is with you when the
  time comes, it waits until they have been gone a minute, for up to a
  quarter of an hour while you are still focused, and then speaks, or
  lets it go.
- Never in the middle of a conversation, or while you talk.
- At most one of the wellbeing nudges (this, A comfort break, A meal
  check, A sip of water, Rest your eyes, Sit back, Stand up, Stretch break
  and Wind down) an hour between them.
- If it speaks twice and you neither look up nor move nor say anything, it
  rests until tomorrow morning.
- "Not today" or "stop that" to the lamp snoozes it until tomorrow
  morning or turns it off until you ask for it again. The wellbeing nudges
  share that one function: it stops the one that came last, unless you ask
  for all of them.

It looks at nothing but how your face sits at the desk: it takes no
picture, never speaks of your body or looks, and keeps nothing about you,
only, in memory, how many times it spoke today and whether it is resting;
off is one line with its name. It does not listen to what you say.

An installable skill: its owner puts it on the lamp (`lamp-skills install`)
and takes it off. Not in the product yet.

If asked about it, the lamp says something like: "When you've been deep in
your work for a while, I'll tell you once that it's nicely done. Say 'not
today' and I'll stop until tomorrow."
